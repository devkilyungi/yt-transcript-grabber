Transcript Grabber, in house style.

Three materials drawn from what the app handles: someone else’s words, typed out, read and marked up. I recommend Marker. Its screens are below in light and dark, at phone and desktop widths, and every frame is the real page, so you can click, tab and press D in it.

Three directions

Scale, structure and behaviour are the kit’s. Surface, accent, texture, display face, mark and splash exit are each direction’s own. None reuses iron, brass, glaze or pen. Every accent sits more than 40° from all four, and every pair passes the contrast test.

  1. Why this material. The product’s whole job is reading, finding and highlighting someone else’s words. A highlighter on typed pages is that job, as an object.
  2. Surface. Cool off-white bond with faint 28px rules, the line pitch of the transcript itself. Dark is the same sheet under a desk lamp: near-black with a plum cast.
  3. Accent. Highlighter pink, #b0177a / #ff5cb8, at 321°, about 56° from iron’s orange, its nearest neighbour. Its four roles stay the kit’s; the highlight wash is a new solid token, --mark.
  4. Display face. Courier Prime Bold: a typewriter face for headings only, so titles read like a typed sheet. Body and numbers stay Atkinson Hyperlegible for misheard auto-caption words and unambiguous 0/O in timestamps.
  5. Mark. A typed page of three lines with a highlighter stroke across the middle one. It draws itself: the lines type on, then the swipe goes over.
  6. Splash exit. Swipe: the cover is wiped off left to right on a slant, with a pink streak on its leading edge, as a highlighter goes across a line.

  2. Surface and accent. Lavender-grey paper with a fibre speckle; duplicator violet #742fb0 / #c58cf2 at 271°.
  3. Display face. Special Elite, a worn typewriter. Charming in headings, but it gets tiring fast and is Apache-licensed rather than OFL.

  5. Against it. Violet sits close to the kit’s blue family on some screens, and the worn face fights the clean reading the product needs.

The motion that matters

Try it in the large frame: click Get transcript, copy, press D, or wait for the spoken paragraph to move. With reduced motion everything still happens, just without the movement.

Open the reading screen

  • Splash: swipe

    760ms in, the mark glides onto the bar’s logo; at 1000ms the cover is wiped away on a slant with a pink edge, 560ms. Once per session, never on reload or with reduced motion; any input skips it.

  • Theme switch

    The kit’s circle: dark spreads out of the toggle in 550ms, light pulls back into it. Press D in any frame.

  • Page change

    Get transcript turns busy in place, then the result arrives as a cross-document view transition: the bar stays, the old page lifts out, blocks rise in.

  • Follow-along

    The spoken paragraph’s margin bar grows in (240ms) as the last one’s shrinks, so the eye can follow the move. Scroll away and Back to now rises in at the bottom.

  • Highlight swipe

    New: a highlight you just made sweeps on left to right in 420ms, like the pen. Saved highlights are simply there.

  • Copy for summary

    The label rolls from Copy to Copied and back after 1.6s; a toast says the same words to screen readers.

  • Player dock

    When the player scrolls out it rises into a small player, bottom right, or a strip under the bar on phones.

  • Rate-limit pause

    A warn notice folds in with a whole-second countdown; when it reaches zero the notice folds away and the queue carries on.

What the spec doesn’t cover

Each designed in the kit’s spirit. If you approve them, they are the parts to fold back into the spec.

Highlight washA new solid token, --mark

The accent has four roles and none is “text background”. Each material gets --mark, a solid wash chosen so ink and muted text on it both pass 4.5:1; the contrast test now checks both pairs.

Find matchesKept apart from highlights

Matches use --well with a 2px ink underline; the current match is solid ink with --on-ink. No accent, so a match never reads as something you highlighted.

The spoken paragraphA running measure

A 3px accent bar in the margin and the time in --accent-text, carried as aria-current. It is a fifth-ish use of the accent, but it is the same role as a selected nav item: “you are here”.

Floating Back to nowA pill over content

The kit has toasts and sheets but no floating action. A raised pill, centred, clear of the home indicator, that only exists while the spoken paragraph is off-screen.

Embedded video and mini playerThird-party media in a house page

The player is YouTube’s and keeps its own chrome; the house frames it with --radius-card and a fixed near-black so it looks the same in both themes. Docked, it is a raised card with pause and close.

Selection popover and notesActing on selected text

A small raised toolbar at the selection (Highlight, Add note, Copy), then a note card anchored to the highlight. Esc closes it and returns focus to the text, as with tips.

Reading measureLong-form text

The kit is built for tools, not reading. Transcript text is 17.5px at 1.7 leading on a 68ch measure, with timestamps hung in a 60px margin; on phones the time sits above its paragraph at 16px.

Queue rows and rate limitsLong-running batch work

One row per video with a status pill (icon and word), the one action that applies, a meter for the whole run, and a warn notice with a countdown when YouTube asks us to slow down.

Third-party hosts in the CSPNamed, and said in the footer
  • frame-src https://www.youtube-nocookie.com for the player
  • script-src https://www.youtube.com for the iframe API, which drives follow-along
  • img-src https://i.ytimg.com for thumbnails

The footer promise says videos play from youtube-nocookie.com, so the claim stays true.

On-device translationA capability that may be missing

Where the browser has the Translator API, “Translate here” runs on the device and says so; where it doesn’t, the option is absent, not disabled with a riddle.

App icon for the macOS shellElectron

The mark on its ink tile at 1024, with the macOS safe area, and a dark variant. Same drawing as the favicon.

Next.js and a strict CSPThe spec prefers no framework for a single-page tool

The spec allows Next 16 for apps, and this one has server routes. I’d keep Next and React, and meet the CSP with a per-request nonce from middleware instead of unsafe-inline.

Two questions before the build

YouTube blocks transcript requests from Vercel

The README says requests from cloud IPs are refused, and step 9 asks for delivery to Vercel. Options: ship the site to Vercel and keep fetching local (desktop app and npm run dev), add a residential proxy as a secret, or fetch in the visitor’s browser through the bookmarklet or an extension.

Keep Next.js

I’d rebuild inside the existing Next 16 app, copying the kit’s CSS and modules in. The CSP would use a per-request nonce rather than a static page. The alternative is a static kit page plus serverless functions.
