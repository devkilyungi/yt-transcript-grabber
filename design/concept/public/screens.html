How bridges hold up: forces for beginners

Field Notes Engineering4:31English, auto-generated

Chapters

6 paragraphs · 712 words · about 3 min to read

Beams

Bridges look still, but every one of them is moving. Traffic, wind and heat push on it all day, and the whole trick is giving those forces somewhere to go.

Start with the simplest kind, a beam. Put a plank across a stream and stand in the middle: the top of the plank squeezes together and the bottom stretches apart.

That squeeze is compression and the stretch is tension. Stone is good at the first and poor at the second, which is why old bridges are arches and not flat slabs.

Arches

An arch turns the weight sideways. Each stone leans on the next, so the load runs around the curve and down into the banks, and the banks have to push back just as hard.

Steel changed the rules because it handles tension well. Now you can hang a road from cables, and the towers only have to carry the weight straight down.

Cables and towers

Which brings us to the question people ask most: why do long bridges sway? The short answer is that they are meant to.
